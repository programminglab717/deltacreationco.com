A beautiful website that doesn't generate enquiries is an expensive brochure. The sites that consistently convert visitors into leads aren't necessarily the flashiest. They're the clearest, the fastest and the most trustworthy.

Here are fifteen principles we build into every website. Most are simple. Few sites apply all of them.

## Clarity first

### 1. Say what you do in five seconds

A visitor should understand what you offer, who it's for and why it matters within seconds of landing. Replace clever-but-vague headlines ("Innovation, reimagined") with specific ones ("Custom websites and automations for growing service businesses").

### 2. Lead with outcomes, not features

Features describe what you do. Outcomes describe what the customer gets. "Automated invoicing" is a feature. "Get paid faster without chasing clients" is an outcome. Headlines and section intros should lead with the outcome and support it with features.

### 3. One page, one primary action

Every page should have a single most-important action, whether that's booking a call, requesting a quote or starting a trial, repeated at natural decision points. Competing calls-to-action split attention and reduce conversions.

### 4. Write for scanners

Most visitors scan before they read. Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs, bullet points and bold key phrases, so someone skimming still gets the message.

## Speed and experience

### 5. Treat speed as a feature

Visitors abandon slow pages, and search engines favor fast ones. Optimize images, minimize JavaScript, use modern frameworks with server rendering, and track Core Web Vitals. Speed improvements are among the most reliable conversion wins available.

### 6. Design mobile-first

For many businesses, most traffic is on phones. Design for the small screen first: thumb-friendly buttons, readable type sizes, short forms and a call-to-action that stays reachable.

### 7. Remove friction from forms

Every extra field costs you enquiries. Ask only for what you need to take the next step. Use smart defaults, clear labels, inline validation and helpful error messages. Consider multi-choice options instead of open text fields where you can.

### 8. Offer a low-commitment next step

Not every visitor is ready to buy. A free consultation, a calculator, a checklist or a demo gives interested but hesitant visitors a way to engage without committing, and gives you a way to continue the conversation.

## Trust and proof

### 9. Show real proof

Specific case studies, named testimonials, recognizable client logos and concrete results build trust far better than generic claims. Place proof near your calls-to-action, where doubt is highest.

### 10. Answer objections before they're asked

Pricing, timelines, process, ownership, support: the questions that stop people from reaching out should be answered on the page. A well-written FAQ section is one of the most underrated conversion tools.

### 11. Make the process tangible

Uncertainty kills conversions. Show exactly what happens after someone gets in touch: what the first call looks like, how long things take and what they'll receive. Clarity about the process reduces perceived risk.

### 12. Look credible in the details

Broken links, typos, outdated copyright years and stock photos signal neglect. Consistent branding, a professional domain email and up-to-date content signal a business that's paying attention.

## Measure and improve

### 13. Track the right events

Page views aren't enough. Track form submissions, bookings, button clicks and scroll depth, and tie them back to traffic sources, so you know which channels and pages actually produce leads.

### 14. Build for search from day one

Technical SEO isn't a bolt-on. Semantic HTML, descriptive titles and meta descriptions, structured data, internal linking, a sitemap and fast performance give every page a fair chance to rank, and organic visitors often convert best because they're actively searching.

### 15. Test, learn and iterate

Your launch is the beginning, not the end. Review analytics monthly, watch session recordings, test headline and offer variations, and keep improving the pages that matter most.

## Putting it into practice

You don't need to fix everything at once. Start with the pages that get the most traffic, usually your homepage and main service pages, and work through this list:

- Is the value proposition instantly clear?
- Is there one obvious next step?
- Does the page load quickly on a phone?
- Is there proof near the call-to-action?
- Are the top objections answered?
